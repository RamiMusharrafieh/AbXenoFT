Macaque training · data integrity

Half a random validation set sits two mutations from training data

Three controls on the framework pipeline: the macaque model's cross-entropy curve, a direct measurement of how much a random split leaks into validation, and the deduplication funnel for both species.

The leakage figure is measured rather than assumed. For a sample of validation sequences we find the Hamming distance to the nearest training sequence under each split scheme: the closer that neighbour, the less the validation score means.

Macaque cross-entropy

Masked-language-model loss is cross-entropy over the 20 amino acids at masked positions. One epoch, 60,083 steps, batch 16, evaluated every 4,000 steps on 10,000 held-out-germline sequences. Falls from 2.347 at initialisation to a best of 0.4427 (perplexity 1.557) at step 60,000, with the learning-rate anneal delivering its gain in the final fifth, the same shape the mouse run showed.

Leakage control: how close is the nearest training sequence?

Exact duplicates cannot leak, because the data is deduplicated. What leaks is near duplicates: somatic-hypermutation variants of the same germline landing on opposite sides of a random split. Each curve is the distribution of Hamming distance from a validation sequence to its nearest training neighbour, over 1,500 validation sequences searched against 150,000 training sequences. A random split piles up at distance 1–2; holding out whole V genes pushes the mass right.

Deduplication funnel

What each raw OAS download becomes. The two species could hardly differ more: mouse collapses to 15.4% of its raw reads, macaque retains 82.4%. Mouse loses most of its volume twice over: a third fails QC (mostly incomplete V(D)J spans), then three quarters of the survivors are exact duplicates of another sequence. Macaque loses almost nothing to either.

Why this matters for the reported scores. The mouse model scored 94.77% on a random split and 90.13% on held-out germlines. The leakage measurement explains the gap concretely: under a random split 50.7% of validation sequences sit within two mutations of a training sequence, versus 4.0% under the V-gene split. The model was largely being asked to recall sequences it had almost already seen. Macaque leaks less under a random split (31.3% within two mutations) because its unsorted-B-cell repertoire is more diverse, the same property that makes 82.4% of its reads unique.

Method: Hamming distance over the fixed 91-residue framework, 1,500 validation sequences against a 150,000-sequence training sample per scheme, seed 42. The V-gene scheme is the exact split each model trained under. Funnel counts come from the extraction logs: raw rows in the OAS download, rows passing the productivity/stop-codon/complete-VDJ filters, then unique concatenated frameworks.
