Full-Epoch Germline Test
Held-out germlines · run to completion

The last 54,000 steps bought 0.10 points

The V-gene-held-out fine-tune previously early-stopped at step 80,000, and that left an open question: was its 90.03% recovery on unseen germlines genuinely converged, or just undertrained with a learning-rate schedule that never finished annealing?

It was resumed from checkpoint-80000, with optimizer, scheduler and RNG state intact, and carried to a full epoch of 134,184 steps with the schedule decaying to zero as originally planned. The extra 2.1 hours moved recovery from 90.03% to 90.13%. The generalisation gap is real and converged, not an artifact of stopping early.

What this corrects. The previous comparison warned that the V-gene model's 90.03% was probably suppressed by undertraining, and that the true figure was likely better. Running the schedule to completion refutes that: 54,184 additional steps produced +0.10 points of recovery and 0.009 of loss. The earlier caveat was wrong, and the 4.6-point gap should be read as the genuine cost of a germline the model has never seen.

Best checkpoint was step 110,000 (loss 0.38531), not the final step, because the anneal's last stretch was flat here, unlike the random-split run whose best landed on its final step. One evaluation took 947 s instead of ~37 s because an unrelated macOS process saturated the CPU; loss values are unaffected.
