Two-Species Framework QC
Post-training QC · mouse and macaque

Both models beat the lookup table. They fail differently.

The macaque framework model gets the same control the mouse one did: a per-position consensus table built from training germlines only, scored on the same masked positions. Macaque clears it by 17.8 points, mouse by 18.7, so neither is a dressed-up frequency table.

But the two diverge sharply at the positions that actually vary, and the reason looks biological rather than technical. Everything below is measured on germlines each model never trained on.

Accuracy along the framework

All 91 positions, both models. The hardest positions in both species sit at the CDR boundaries. For macaque the two worst are position 41 (last residue of FR2, abutting CDR2) at 24.4% and position 42 (first residue of FR3, the other side of CDR2) at 39.9%. Mouse bottoms out at position 41 too, but only falls to 43.7%. These are Vernier-zone residues whose identity is coupled to CDRs this dataset removes, so neither model has the context to infer them, and macaque suffers more.

The likely reason macaque struggles at variable positions. The mouse data is sorted naive B cells, where variation between sequences is largely germline-encoded: different V genes, learnable structure. The macaque data is unsorted B cells, so its variable positions carry somatic hypermutation on top of germline variation, and SHM is close to irreducible noise. The same difference shows up upstream: deduplication kept 85% of macaque sequences but only 24% of mouse, and macaque's mean positional conservation is higher (0.803 vs 0.703) while its consensus control scores no better. That pattern, more conserved on average yet harder where it varies, is what a hypermutated repertoire looks like. Testing it would mean re-running on an IgM naive-sorted macaque set.

Method: 4,000 held-out sequences × 13 masked positions = 52,000 predictions per species, 15% masking, top-1. Framework regions are fixed-length (25/17/38/11 = 91); 97.4% of macaque and ~100% of mouse sequences are canonical length and the rest are excluded. Each consensus table is built only from that species' training germlines, giving it the same information its model had. Source: analysis_qc.py --species macaque.
